Tips

Explore general educational information about menstrual health and common conditions.

Understanding the Menstrual Cycle

A menstrual cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period. Bleeding, cycle timing, and symptoms can vary. Tracking dates over several cycles can help you describe your own pattern, but estimates are not guarantees.

Common Period Symptoms

Cramps, bloating, headaches, breast tenderness, tiredness, and mood changes can occur around menstruation. Their intensity and timing differ between people. If symptoms repeatedly disrupt school, work, sleep, or daily activities, discuss them with a qualified healthcare professional.

PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)

PCOS is a hormonal condition that can affect ovulation and may be associated with irregular or missed periods, acne, increased facial or body hair, or changes in how the body uses insulin. Experiences vary, and these signs can have other causes. PCOS cannot be confirmed from a symptom list or period tracker; assessment by a qualified healthcare professional is needed.

Endometriosis

Endometriosis is a condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus grows outside the uterus. Possible symptoms include significant period pain, ongoing pelvic pain, pain during bowel movements or urination around periods, and difficulty becoming pregnant. Some people have few symptoms. Symptoms alone do not confirm endometriosis, so seek professional assessment for persistent or disruptive pain.

PMS and PMDD

Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) describes physical or emotional symptoms that occur before a period and improve after it begins. Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a more severe condition that can substantially affect mood and daily life. Recording when symptoms start and ease can help a healthcare professional assess them.

When to seek medical advice

Consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional if periods become persistently irregular, bleeding is unusually heavy, pain is severe or worsening, symptoms affect daily activities, or you have concerns about your cycle. Seek urgent medical care for sudden severe pain, fainting, or heavy bleeding with weakness or dizziness, especially if you may be pregnant.

Using HerHealth

Record what you experience and, when possible, note when symptoms begin and ease. Reviewing entries over time can help you describe changes and recurring patterns.
